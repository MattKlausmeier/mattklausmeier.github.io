Do You Give God Your Gory?

November 25, 2026

... For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.

(Matt 6:13b KJV)

(While Biblical scholars disagree about whether the above verse was part of the original Biblical manuscripts, all agree that the kingdom, the power, and the glory belong to God.)

To glorify is to praise, extol, magnify, honor, and celebrate. In short, to make much of something. What are you making much of? What do you magnify, honor, and celebrate? Apple just released a new feature on their watches which automatically listens to everything you say throughout the day, so you can get a summary or reminder later on. If we asked your Apple Watch who you gave the most glory to throughout the last week, who or what would it be?

This entire universe is God's handiwork - His masterpiece - and we are a part of that. And while some works of art convey messages of love, bravery, and beauty, all of them also give glory to their creator. But unlike any paint ever applied to canvas, we are a work of art which can choose to be what we were created to be - giving God glory - or we can rebel and rob Him of His glory.

Giving God glory isn't just about singing church songs and quoting scripture. We were made in the image and likeness of God (Gen 1:26), and when we live godly lives and live according to His nature we glorify Him. This means telling the truth, working hard, dealing honestly with people, loving our neighbors, being faithful in marriage, raising our children well, and whatever other virtue you can bring to mind. It means walking according to God's Word, which acts as our compass for all those times that sin and the world make us confused as to how to live.

We also glorify Him when, in those moments when is praising us, "passing on" the glory to Him. We see examples of this when an Oscar winner praises God when receiving their statue, or when a football player "Tebows" in the end zone. Anytime you could say, "You're welcome," you can instead say, "Jesus loves you!" (I once explained this concept to my youth group kids, and from then on they would fight for the chance to open the door for people, so that they could respond, "Jesus loves you!" when anyone thanked them.)

Anything you do can be done in a way that either honors God or dishonors Him. It doesn't have to be super religious; it might just be responding with kindness, or giving someone who hurt you another chance. But when people notice your goodness, don't take their praise for yourself; make sure they know that God is the reason you are the way you are. "Therefore, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all things for the glory of God." (1Cor 10:31 NASB)

Consider:
  • If we asked your friends, family, and coworkers (or your Apple Watch!) what you think of God, what would their response be?
  • Do you have any behaviors you might do differently if Jesus was standing next to you?
  • If making these changes is difficult, might you be struggling with your own perception of God's goodness? Develop a habit of hearing and remembering the good things he has done.
