Time to Take the Field

October 16, 2026

Then the Lord God said, "It is not good for the man to be alone..."

(Gen 2:18 NASB)

After yesterday's devotional, you might think that the best way for a Christian to live is to completely unplug from everyone and everything, and go live as a hermit in the woods, enjoying God's presence as your only delight and comfort. But God Himself says that this is "not good" for us. We were made to live in relationships with other people, including a spouse, children, relatives, neighbors, coworkers, and our Christian family.

Even those in the Bible who spent time alone were eventually brought back into civilization to complete God's work (Moses, Elijah, John the Baptist, Jesus...). God uses you to build up others, and He uses others to build you up. All throughout the Bible we read stories of what appears to be ordinary life coincidences turning into divine appointments (Philip and the Ethiopian, Moses and Pharaoh's daughter, and pretty much Joseph's entire life). All of the days ordained for you were written in God's book before one had even come to pass (Ps 139:16), and it is because of this foreknowledge God can put you with exactly the people you need, and the people who need you.

God wants us to have those special, exclusive moments with Him every day, just as Jesus started each day going off to spend time alone with His heavenly Father (Luke 5:16, Mark 1:35). And while remaining heavenly minded throughout the day will ensure we stay on the straight and narrow path, these moments with God should be like our meals: we sit down, enjoy them, become nourished and satisfied, but then use that nutrition as the strength we need to go back out into the world and accomplish our work, both kingdom work and our material efforts. It would be worthless for basketball players to stay in the coach's huddle and not play the game. It would be destructive for someone to sit at a table all day eating and never put that energy to any use. And it's God's design for us to come to Him for our spiritual bread, our heavenly coaching, and then go back into the world where we put it all to good use.

Consider:
  • Do you find yourself trying to escape the world God has placed you into?
  • Do you regularly remind yourself that the most ordinary events of your day might be God's "divine appointments" for you?
  • If your attitude is that you're "too good" to be mixing with "the likes of them", remember that those are exactly the kind of "sinners" Jesus would spend time with. Ask Jesus to forgive you for and deliver you from pride.
