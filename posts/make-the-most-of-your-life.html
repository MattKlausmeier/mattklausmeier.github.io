Make the Most of Your Life

October 5, 2026

But [Jesus] said to them, "Beware, and be on your guard against every form of greed; for not even when one is affluent does his life consist of his possessions." And He told them a parable, saying, "The land of a rich man was very productive. And he began thinking to himself, saying, 'What shall I do, since I have no place to store my crops?' And he said, 'This is what I will do: I will tear down my barns and build larger ones, and I will store all my grain and my goods there. And I will say to myself, "You have many goods stored up for many years to come; relax, eat, drink, and enjoy yourself!"' But God said to him, 'You fool! This very night your soul is demanded of you; and as for all that you have prepared, who will own it now?' Such is the one who stores up treasure for himself, and is not rich in relation to God."

(Luke 12:15-21 NASB)

I've been keeping the idea for this blog at the back of my mind for at least a year now, but as I contemplated the meaning of this parable, I realized that I need to get started on it today. Many have misinterpreted this passage to say that Jesus was preaching against wealth. But his actual target was a life lived for self rather than for heaven, and that's a trap many of us will fall into during our time on earth.

The man in this story had more than enough. He was at a place where he no longer needed to work, and could have devoted his remaining time, talents, and resources to the kingdom. But instead of using the freedom provided by his prosperity to attend to higher matters, he instead chose to retire and live only for himself. He did not realize that his time was up, and that once he stood before the Lord, his earthly treasure would stay behind, leaving him a pauper in heaven.

So often we tell kids to "start saving early", but we fail to take our own advice when it comes to saving up treasure in heaven. I'm starting this blog because I have the time and spiritual gift to do it. I'm still tempted to focus on improving my earthly situation, but when I think about my blessings with a humble heart, I realize I have plenty. So instead I want to encourage and strengthen you, rather than spending every waking minute pursuing a "better life". As Jesus said, "...not even when one is affluent does his life consist of his possessions." In other words, life is more than money.

Consider:
  • What kingdom opportunities could you make time for in your life right now?
  • What gifts do you have that could bring the lost into the church, or strengthen and encourage your brothers and sisters in Christ?
  • If God rewarded you for the things you've done *for* Him (not just the sins you didn't commit), what kind of reward would you expect to receive?
