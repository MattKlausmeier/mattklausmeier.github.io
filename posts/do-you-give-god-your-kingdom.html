Do You Give God Your Kingdom?

November 23, 2026

... For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.

(Matt 6:13b KJV)

(While Biblical scholars disagree about whether the above verse was part of the original Biblical manuscripts, all agree that the kingdom, the power, and the glory belong to God.)

A kingdom is the realm under the authority of a king. A person might be under the authority of one king one day, and a different king the next. Early America was under the authority of King George III, until the people who lived there decided it would no longer be. And George III ruled England until January 29, 1820, the day he died. The next day, England was under the reign of George IV. It's not the land or the people or the buildings which changed, merely the authority.

Who has the ultimate authority in your life? When you're presented with the opportunity to choose, before whom do you kneel and say, "Your will be done?" Some areas of our lives are much easier to submit to God than others. Maybe it's easy to submit your Sunday mornings to going to church, but you resist allowing Him to say who you'll be in a relationship with. Maybe when He says, "Do not lie," you find it easy, but perhaps you give a little too much truth out when He says, "Do not gossip."

God has the right to our total submission; there is no corner of our lives which we can rightfully hold back from His claim. "There is not a square inch in the whole domain of our human existence over which Christ, who is Sovereign over all, does not cry: 'Mine!'" (Abraham Kuyper, Sphere Sovereignty) If you're holding back a part of your kingdom today, it's time to lay it down at the foot of the cross.

Consider:
  • Are there any areas of your life where you refuse to do things God's way? Whoever has the final say is the true lord of your life.
  • Do you struggle to believe and obey the "outdated" parts of the Bible? Our God is the same yesterday, today, and forever.
  • Are you worried people might call you a "zealot", "radical", or "extremist"? This is how Jesus and His disciples were seen.
