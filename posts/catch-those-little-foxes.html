Catch Those Little Foxes!

October 25, 2026

"Catch the foxes for us, The little foxes that are ruining the vineyards..."

(Song 2:15a NASB)

Most scholars agree that the vineyards in this verse represent the lovers' relationship, and the little foxes are those things which damage their relationship. Our God - who is Himself the perfect relationship, existing as the Trinity - created us to walk in perfect love with Him and others. And we would have no problem walking in this love except for the fall of mankind in the garden. Now we find the very thing we were created for to be a constant battle.

This is why we ought to concern ourselves with "catching" the little things that spoil our relationships. Yesterday we talked about how the fruit of the Spirit aids us in our relationships, but now we must consider what we are allowing to run free which hinders them.

In our relationship with God, the little foxes can be little sins, things we dismiss as being "no big deal", but which God wants us to be rid of. A great example of this is "little white lies". The Bible teaches that the devil is the father of lies (John 8:44) and that "all liars" will be cast into hell (Rev 21:8), yet we casually toss falsehoods around out of fear, or to avoid difficult conversations. Or maybe it's gossip, which is like a delicious "dainty morsel" (Prov 18:8) to those who share it, but which Paul lists as a sin alongside wickedness, greed, and murder. (Rom 1:29)

The other little foxes that might spoil your relationships with both God and others are the "harmless" ways we waste our time, good things which rob of us the best things. For example, there's certainly no prohibition in the Bible against playing video games, and it can be a fun way to pass some time, but when we choose to do that rather than to spending time in prayer, or loving our spouses, or nurturing our children, we rob ourselves and our loved ones of something they deserve more than your right to have fun.

We were created in the image of a relational God to be relational people. Let's examine those relationships and catch any little foxes that might spoil them.

Consider:
  • Do you allow yourself to commit "little" sins, saying, "Everybody does it," or, "It's no big deal"?
  • Do you have hobbies or distractions that are hurting your relationships, your job, or your usefulness to the Kingdom?
  • Ask God to make you aware of anything He doesn't want in your life, anything ruining your relationship with Him or others.
