It All Must Come From Love

November 21, 2026

and you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.

(Mark 12:30 NASB)

The true Christian walk is inseparable from loving God. It is the first and greatest commandment. Any form of religion we undertake which comes from fear, duty, tradition, or anything other than love is not Christianity. In 1 Corinthians 13, the love chapter, Paul describes a man performing enormous acts of spiritual power and charity, and yet says such a person's life is wasted if all of that is done without love. Love towards the recipient of those actions, yes, but that only fulfills the second commandment; it's love towards God, motivating those actions, which makes them truly valuable.

Without love, we are just actors pretending to be Christian. Without love, we are those about whom God says, "This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far away from Me." (Matt 15:8 NASB) Too many churches have preached the message of focusing on the outward without performing the more important duty of the inward. But Jesus says, "I desire compassion, rather than sacrifice." (Matt 9:13) When we recognize that the greatest act of love God could ever perform was already done for us back when we were still sinners (Rom 5:8), we can abandon the lie that there's a greater love we can get from Him. We can become completely satisfied with this greatest love (John 15:13) and simply love Him in return. Out of our love will naturally flow the life He calls us to live.

But what about when the Bible talks about the "fear of the Lord"? The fear of the Lord is not the same thing as a fear of losing God's love or our salvation. Fear occurs when we greatly care about something. We all care about our own wellbeing, causing us to have fear of those things which would harm us. Or we might care for our children's health, giving us fear of where they might go or of what they're putting in their mouths. When we begin to care about God and His concerns, and about our relationship with Him, we rightly learn to fear those things which upset Him, which go against His desires, and which hinder our relationship with Him. We fear when we care; to care is to love.

The next time you're praying, ask yourself how easy it is to say, "I love you, Lord". If you struggle to express your love like this, you might be caught up in a religion motivated by something other than love. You might be trying to earn something good - or avoid something bad - from God. Lay down your obligations and truly believe that God will not - cannot - ever love you more than He does right now. Let His love for you light up your heart, and turn that light of love back towards Him. Only then will you be able to live the true life of a Christian. And if you're struggling to build up that love, you can freely pray, "God, help me love you more!" That's a prayer He would love to answer for you!

Consider:
  • How easy is it for you to feel love towards God? Do you feel delight towards Him? Do you long for His eyes to turn towards you?
  • We love God more when we remember all the good He has done in the past and will do in the future. What can you do to better hold those good deeds in the forefront of your mind?
  • Does a false doctrine of works-based salvation rob you of living freely in His love? Repent of such false beliefs and enjoy the freedom of a religion that was finished on the cross.
